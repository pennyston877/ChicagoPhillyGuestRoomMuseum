☆ The Miracle of Game 4

Down 8–0 going into the bottom of the 7th inning of Game 4, the Philadelphia Athletics staged the greatest comeback in World Series history — scoring 10 runs in a single inning to win 10–8. It remains one of the most shocking turnarounds in baseball history nearly 100 years later.

About this piece

This is the oldest piece in the collection by nearly two decades — a Los Angeles Times rotogravure section from October 1929, documenting the World Series between the Philadelphia Athletics and the Chicago Cubs. The rotogravure printing process gave newspapers of the era their distinctive sepia-toned, high-quality photographic images, making these sections the closest thing to sports photography that existed for most Americans.

The 1929 World Series is one of the most famous in baseball history, remembered above all for Game 4's miracle comeback. The Cubs led 8–0 going into the bottom of the 7th. Twenty minutes later the Athletics had scored 10 runs and led 10–8. They won the game, then the Series.

This piece was acquired through Heritage Auctions, one of the world's premier sports memorabilia auction houses. At nearly 100 years old, it is a document of a city that didn't exist yet as a major sports town — Los Angeles in 1929 had no MLB team, no NFL team, nothing. Just newspapers covering the national pastime from 2,000 miles away. And yet here it is, on a wall in Chicago, connecting two cities and one great rivalry across a century.
