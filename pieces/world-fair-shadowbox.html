A rare accordion-style photographic souvenir booklet from the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago — one of the most significant events in American history. The fair introduced electric lights to millions of Americans for the first time, and its grand "White City" of neoclassical buildings on the Chicago lakefront became one of the most photographed places on earth. Printed in Germany and published in the U.S., this booklet contains historical views of the fair's architecture and exhibits.

1876 Philadelphia Centennial Exhibition Fan
Philadelphia
Year 1876
Location Fairmount Park, Philadelphia
Format Paper folding fan souvenir
Decoration Main building + Asian motifs

An antique paper folding fan from the 1876 Philadelphia Centennial Exhibition — America's first World's Fair, held to celebrate the country's 100th birthday. One side features the main exhibition building in Fairmount Park; the other features Asian decorative motifs, reflecting the international character of the fair. Nearly 150 years old and still intact. Philadelphia hosted the world before Chicago did.

Confetti from Super Bowl LIX — the Philadelphia Eagles defeating the Kansas City Chiefs for the championship. This wasn't bought or collected; it was given by a great friend who was there. It sat on that field. It fell from that ceiling. It's the newest thing in the oldest frame in the collection, tucked in the corner of a shadow box next to an 1876 fan and an 1893 booklet. Philadelphia, then and now.

About this piece

This shadow box is the most conceptually ambitious piece in the collection. Three objects, 149 years apart, from the two cities at the center of everything on this wall. Chicago hosted the world in 1893. Philadelphia hosted it in 1876 — 17 years earlier. And in 2025, Philadelphia won the Super Bowl, and a friend brought back a handful of confetti from the field.

One frame. Two cities. A century and a half of history, compressed into a shadow box hanging in a Chicago apartment. It's the whole point of the wall, in miniature.
