☆ The Last Game at the Spectrum

On March 14, 2009, the Philadelphia 76ers hosted the Chicago Bulls in the final NBA game ever played at the Spectrum. The arena that had been home to Philadelphia sports for 42 years — two Flyers Stanley Cups, 76ers championships, and decades of unforgettable moments — played its last game against the Bulls. Then they cut up the floor and sold it.

✓ Certificate of Authenticity included · Edition 1749 of 4500 · Official Philadelphia 76ers Spectrum Court
About this piece

This is a literal piece of the Spectrum — a section of hardwood from the actual court where the 76ers played their final game, cut up and preserved after the building closed. The plaque on the piece reads exactly what it is: Philadelphia 76ers vs Chicago Bulls, March 14, 2009, edition 1749 of 4500.

The Spectrum opened in 1967 and became one of the most beloved arenas in Philadelphia sports history. The Flyers won Stanley Cups there in 1974 and 1975. The 76ers won a championship there in 1983. Dr. J played there. Charles Barkley played there. Allen Iverson played there. For 42 years it was the beating heart of Philadelphia sports.

The last team they hosted was the Chicago Bulls. Of course it was. One final Philly vs Chicago game, and then the lights went out. This piece of floor was under someone's feet for that last game — it absorbed 42 years of history, and now it's on a wall in Chicago. It doesn't get more fitting than that.
