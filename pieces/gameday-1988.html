About this piece

This is the program from one of the most legendary and bizarre games in NFL playoff history — the Fog Bowl. On New Year's Eve 1988, a dense fog rolled off Lake Michigan and blanketed Soldier Field so completely that players, coaches, and fans could barely see the field. Television cameras were useless. Referees struggled to spot the ball. The game was played almost entirely by feel.

Chicago won 20–12, but nobody who was there will tell you the score was the story. The story was the fog — thick, grey, absolute. Players from both teams have said it was the strangest thing they ever experienced on a football field. Randall Cunningham was nearly invisible in the mist. Reggie White was a ghost rushing out of the grey. Mike Ditka was a silhouette on the sideline.

The program was printed before any of that happened. It has the rosters, the lineups, the ads — a perfectly normal game program for what turned out to be one of the most abnormal games ever played. New Year's Eve. Soldier Field. The fog. Bears win.
