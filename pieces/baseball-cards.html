★ Family connection

Lee Elia is the second cousin of the owner's grandfather. The link runs through a shared Albanian heritage and a Philadelphia family name: both Lee Elia's mother and the owner's great-grandmother share the maiden name Soulas — an Albanian surname rooted in Philadelphia's tight-knit immigrant community.

About this piece

This framed set of three signed cards tells the story of Lee Elia's baseball life — and it happens to touch every corner of this gallery's theme. Born and raised in Philadelphia, Elia played shortstop for the Chicago White Sox in 1966 (80 games) and the Chicago Cubs in 1968 (15 games), before transitioning into coaching and managing. He managed the Cubs from 1982 to 1983, then returned to his hometown to manage the Phillies from 1987 to 1988. He also served as third base coach for the Phillies' 1980 World Series championship team — one of the greatest moments in Philadelphia sports history.

White Sox. Cubs. Phillies. His career is practically a map of this collection.

Elia spent over 50 years in professional baseball across 10 organizations, coaching for the Yankees, Mariners, Blue Jays, Rays, and Orioles among others. He passed away in July 2025 at the age of 87. The Phillies said in their statement that he "always considered himself a Phillie at heart."

Elia is perhaps best remembered for one of the most legendary tirades in baseball history — a profanity-laced press conference rant after a Cubs loss in April 1983 that became an instant piece of sports folklore. But beyond the rant, he was a respected baseball lifer who went on to coach for multiple organizations over decades.

Three cards. Three chapters. One family name connecting Philadelphia's Albanian community across generations. This is the most personal piece on the wall.
