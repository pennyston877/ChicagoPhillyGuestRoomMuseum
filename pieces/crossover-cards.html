The Crossover Cards

Players who wore both cities. Some were legends on one side and barely a footnote on the other. Some crossed over at the peak of their careers. All of them belong on this wall.

⚾ Card arriving soon En route
Jimmy Rollins
MLB · Shortstop
Phillies → White Sox

15 seasons as a Phillie, 3-time All-Star, 2007 NL MVP, and the heartbeat of the 2008 World Series championship team. His brief stint with the White Sox in 2016 was the footnote at the end of a Philly legend's career.

⚾ Card arriving soon En route
John Kruk
MLB · First Baseman
Phillies → White Sox

One of the most beloved Phillies of his generation — a fan favorite who played with grit, humor, and a left-handed swing that looked wrong and worked perfectly. His 1994 White Sox stint lasted just 45 games before illness ended his career.

🏒 Card arriving soon En route
Patrick Sharp
NHL · Left Wing
Phantoms → Blackhawks

Sharp came up through the Flyers organization and played for the Philadelphia Phantoms — the AHL affiliate — before being traded to Chicago, where he became a three-time Stanley Cup champion and one of the most popular Blackhawks of the dynasty era.

🏈 Card arriving soon En route
Jason Peters
NFL · Offensive Tackle
Eagles → Bears

One of the greatest offensive linemen in Eagles history — a two-time All-Pro and cornerstone of Philadelphia's offensive line for over a decade, including the 2017 Super Bowl run. His brief Bears stint in 2021 was the final chapter of a Hall of Fame-caliber career.

🏈 Card arriving soon En route
Nick Foles
NFL · Quarterback
Eagles → Bears

Super Bowl LII MVP. The man who beat the Patriots when nobody thought it was possible. Foles signed with Chicago in 2020, becoming a Bear after being a Philadelphia legend — a crossover that still feels strange to type.

🏈 Card arriving soon En route
Mike Ditka
NFL · Tight End / Head Coach
Eagles ↔ Bears

The ultimate crossover. Ditka was drafted by the Bears in 1961, became a Hall of Fame tight end, was traded to the Eagles in 1967 for two seasons — then returned to Chicago as head coach and won Super Bowl XX in 1985. Philadelphia and Chicago, player and coach. Nobody belongs on this wall more than Iron Mike.
