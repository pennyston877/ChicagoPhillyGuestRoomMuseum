The Palestra is the most storied college basketball arena in America — a 1927 gymnasium at the University of Pennsylvania that became the spiritual home of Philadelphia basketball. Five Big 5 schools, one building, decades of the most intense college hoops anywhere.

The teams
Philadelphia
Temple Owls
One of Philadelphia's Big 5 programs, with a proud basketball tradition going back decades. Temple at the Palestra was always a home game in spirit.
Philadelphia
La Salle Explorers
La Salle was the powerhouse of the early Big 5 era — Tom Gola led them to the 1954 NCAA championship, and by 1965 the program was still one of the most respected in the country.
Philadelphia
Villanova Wildcats
A Big 5 mainstay and perennial NCAA contender. Villanova basketball at the Palestra was electric — one of the great atmospheres in college sports.

Chicago's representative
DePaul brought Chicago to the Palestra — one of the great Catholic basketball schools of the Midwest, making the trip east to face Philadelphia's finest.
About this piece

This program was hidden inside one of the frames on the wall — part of the secret stash. A 1965 Palestra Illustrated program for a doubleheader featuring three Philadelphia Big 5 schools and DePaul from Chicago. Even the secret items follow the theme.

The Palestra in 1965 was at the peak of its powers as the cathedral of Philadelphia basketball. The Big 5 — Penn, Temple, La Salle, Villanova, and Saint Joseph's — played their home games there and faced each other in a round-robin series that was the most intense rivalry in college hoops. A DePaul appearance made it a true Philadelphia-Chicago matchup.
