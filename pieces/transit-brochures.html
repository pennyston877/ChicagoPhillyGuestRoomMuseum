1954 Chicago Transit Authority Map
Chicago
Year 1954
Issued by Chicago Transit Authority
Coverage Streetcar, Bus, "L" & Subway
Also shows Connecting suburban bus routes

A 1954 CTA system map showing Chicago's full transit network at its mid-century peak — the famous "L" elevated lines, the streetcars that would soon be retired, the subway, and the buses. The CTA was formed in 1947 by consolidating the city's private transit companies, and this map is from the early years of that unified system. Chicago's "L" is one of the oldest rapid transit systems in the world, and this map captures it before the city began tearing out streetcar lines in the late 1950s.

1965 Pennsylvania Railroad / SEPTA Timetable
Philadelphia
Effective date October 31, 1965
Route Chestnut Hill — Philadelphia
Operation "Operation Northwest"
Logos Pennsylvania Railroad + SEPTA
Stations Chestnut Hill, Highland, St. Martins, Allen Lane & more
Transfer PTC Bus connections listed

A 1965 Pennsylvania Railroad timetable for the Chestnut Hill line — one of Philadelphia's most beloved commuter rail routes, running through the leafy northwest neighborhoods of the city. The timetable sits at a fascinating moment in Philadelphia transit history: the Pennsylvania Railroad still operates the line, but the SEPTA logo already appears, signaling the transition to the public authority that would take over regional rail in the years ahead. "Operation Northwest" is printed at the top — a very 1960s way of naming a train schedule.

About this piece

Two cities, two transit systems, two different eras of American urban life — framed together. The 1954 CTA map and the 1965 SEPTA timetable are the kind of objects that were printed by the millions and saved by almost nobody. They were functional documents: you used them to get somewhere, then threw them away. The fact that either of these survived is a small miracle of hoarding and luck.

Together they capture something real about Chicago and Philadelphia — two cities built around dense, walkable neighborhoods connected by transit long before the car took over. The "L" still runs. The Chestnut Hill line still runs. The cities are still there. These papers from 1954 and 1965 are proof that they always were.
